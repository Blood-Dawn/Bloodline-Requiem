[b]ISABELLA CRIMSON[/b]
[i]Viscountess of the Crimson Court, Voice of the Matriarch, The Velvet Blade[/i]

[b]Public Face:[/b]
Isabella Crimson is the Matriarch's primary diplomat—the face the Varkyr court presents to other factions. She is known for charm, eloquence, and an uncanny ability to find compromise where none seems possible. In two centuries of service, she has prevented three potential wars, negotiated dozens of territorial disputes, and maintained relationships that benefit her faction without obviously disadvantaging others.

She is also known for her beauty, her grace, and her carefully cultivated air of harmlessness. Many have made the mistake of underestimating her.

[b]The Truth:[/b]
Isabella chose diplomacy not from weakness but from calculation. She observed, during her early centuries, that Varkyr nobles who relied purely on strength tended to die violently. Those who cultivated relationships—who made themselves useful to multiple parties—survived.

She is not harmless. She is not soft. She is a predator who has chosen to hunt through words rather than claws, and her prey rarely realizes they've been caught until they're already drained.

*if (learned_isabella_origin)
    [b]Origin:[/b]
    Isabella was born human in what is now Veilfall, during the final century of the Millennium War. Her family were Varkyr collaborators—not thralls, but willing servants who traded service for protection.
    
    She was Embraced at twenty-three by a Count who saw her potential. What she remembers most clearly from her human life is hunger: the desperation of wartime scarcity, the competition for scraps, the willingness to do anything for survival.
    
    That hunger never left her. It merely evolved. She no longer hungers for food; she hungers for security, for influence, for the certainty that she will never be vulnerable again.

*if (learned_isabella_secrets)
    [b]What She Hides:[/b]
    
    [i]The Crimson Eclipse Connection[/i] — Isabella's sire was executed as a Crimson Eclipse sympathizer after the war. This connection has been erased from official records, but some in the court know. She has spent two centuries proving her loyalty, knowing that a single misstep could resurrect old suspicions.
    
    [i]The Lupine Contact[/i] — She maintains a secret friendship with a Lupine diplomat, exchanging information that benefits both factions. This is technically treason; both would be executed if discovered.
    
    [i]The Escape Plan[/i] — She has resources hidden outside Veilfall—blood supplies, gold, false identities. If the political winds ever shift against her, she will vanish overnight. She has never needed this plan; she has never stopped maintaining it.

*if (learned_isabella_relationships)
    [b]Key Relationships:[/b]
    
    [i]The Matriarch[/i] — Isabella is genuinely loyal, not from affection but from calculated self-interest. The Matriarch protects her; the Matriarch gives her purpose; without the Matriarch, Isabella would have to find another patron or become powerful enough to need none.
    
    [i]Vincent Ashenheart[/i] — Complicated. They were lovers once, centuries ago. The relationship ended when Isabella chose advancement over him. They remain professional; the old feelings remain buried but not dead.
    
    [i]Cassandra Nightfall[/i] — Mutual respect, mutual wariness. Cassandra represents the martial power Isabella deliberately rejected. Each sees in the other the path they didn't take.

[b]ISABELLA'S AGENDA[/b]

[i]Analysis based on observed behavior patterns[/i]

Isabella's immediate goals align with her public role: maintain peace, preserve Varkyr interests, prevent another Millennium War. These are genuine objectives; she believes in them.

Her deeper goals are more personal:

[i]Security[/i] — She never wants to feel vulnerable again. Every alliance, every favor, every carefully maintained relationship is a brick in the wall between her and danger.

[i]Advancement[/i] — Viscountess is not her final destination. She believes she could serve the Matriarch better with greater authority. A Countess can do things a Viscountess cannot.

[i]Legacy[/i] — She has no childer, no proteges, no one to continue her work after she's gone. This troubles her more than she admits. She wants to leave something permanent—a treaty, an institution, a tradition—that will outlast her.

[i]The Hidden Goal[/i] — She wants to know what the Matriarch keeps in the sealed chamber. Not from curiosity; from the need to know everything that could affect her security. This secret is the one gap in her intelligence network, and it haunts her.

[b]VINCENT ASHENHEART[/b]
[i]Count of the Ashenheart Line, The Silent Death, Shadow of the Matriarch[/i]

[b]Public Face:[/b]
Vincent Ashenheart is the Matriarch's enforcer—the vampire who handles problems that cannot be solved through diplomacy. His reputation precedes him: cold, efficient, unstoppable. In four centuries, he has never failed an assignment.

He speaks rarely and smiles never. He attends court functions only when required, leaves as soon as possible, and spends most of his time in solitary pursuits that no one dares question.

[b]The Truth:[/b]
Vincent is not cold. He is controlled. The difference matters.

Beneath the mask of professional detachment lives someone who feels deeply—too deeply, by Varkyr standards. He has learned to suppress emotion because his emotions, if released, would destroy him. The one time he allowed himself to feel freely, it nearly ended in his true death.

He kills because he is good at it, because it is his function, because every target eliminated is a problem solved. He takes no pleasure in the act. He simply... does it, then moves on, never dwelling, never questioning.

The questions would break him if he started asking them.

*if (learned_vincent_origin)
    [b]Origin:[/b]
    Vincent was Embraced during the Millennium War's middle centuries—not as a reward, but as a punishment.
    
    He had been a human warrior, fighting for a lord who allied with the Lupines. He was captured, tortured, and offered a choice: join the Varkyrs or watch his family die.
    
    He chose to join.
    
    They killed his family anyway.
    
    His sire laughed as Vincent's human life was erased, telling him that this was his first lesson: Varkyrs do not negotiate. They take what they want.
    
    Vincent learned that lesson. He killed his sire three decades later—slowly, methodically, savoring every moment. It was the last time he allowed himself to feel anything deeply.

*if (learned_vincent_secrets)
    [b]What He Hides:[/b]
    
    [i]The Count of Mercy[/i] — Vincent has a reputation for clean kills, quick and professional. What no one knows is that he offers every target a chance to surrender. Most refuse, not recognizing mercy when it's offered. The few who accept... he relocates them, erases their identities, gives them new lives. Officially, they died. The Matriarch has never questioned his efficiency.
    
    [i]The Memory Palace[/i] — In his mind, Vincent maintains a perfect record of every face, every name, every death. He could tell you about each of his kills in precise detail. He does this because forgetting would be easy, and he refuses to let himself take the easy path.
    
    [i]Isabella[/i] — He still loves her. He will never act on it, never speak of it, never allow it to influence his decisions. But it's there, a wound that never healed, a weakness he guards more carefully than any secret.

*if (learned_vincent_skills)
    [b]Capabilities:[/b]
    Vincent is considered one of the most dangerous individual combatants in Veilfall. His known abilities include:
    
    [i]Shadow Mastery[/i] — Exceptional even by Varkyr standards. He can travel through shadows instantaneously, create shadow constructs, and render himself effectively invisible in any darkness.
    
    [i]Perfect Memory[/i] — He remembers everything. Faces, conversations, combat patterns. This makes him impossible to deceive with the same trick twice.
    
    [i]Combat Precognition[/i] — Not true foresight, but a combination of experience and enhanced perception that allows him to anticipate attacks before they occur.
    
    [i]The Thousand Cuts[/i] — His signature technique: a flurry of strikes too fast to track, each targeting a different vital point. Victims rarely realize they've been hit until they begin falling apart.

[b]VINCENT'S CONNECTIONS[/b]

[i]Assessment of loyalties and relationships[/i]

[b]The Matriarch:[/b]
Vincent is genuinely loyal—not from manipulation or obligation, but because the Matriarch saw something in him worth saving. When he first joined the court, traumatized and purposeless, she gave him a role: her executioner. She made him valuable. She gave him meaning.

He would die for her. He would kill for her. He has done both, many times.

[b]Isabella Crimson:[/b]
The one wound that never healed. They met when Vincent was still learning to suppress his emotions and Isabella was still climbing the political ladder. For fifty years, they were... something. Not quite lovers, not quite partners. Something between.

Then Isabella chose advancement over him, using information he'd shared to secure a promotion that left him behind. She apologized, years later. He forgave her, honestly.

The love didn't go away. It just became another thing to bury.

[b]The Assassin Guild:[/b]
Vincent maintains a professional relationship with the Guild—not membership, but mutual respect. They don't compete; their methods and targets rarely overlap. Occasionally, they exchange information.

Thorne, the current Guild Master, is one of the few people Vincent considers something like a friend.

[b]The Target:[/b]
This is the relationship Vincent never discusses.

Every assassin, he believes, has one kill they never forget—one target who changes them. For Vincent, it was a human woman, three centuries ago. She was targeted for knowing too much about Varkyr operations.

He offered her the chance to surrender. She refused, but not from pride. She refused because she was protecting someone else—a child, hidden in the walls, who would die if Vincent searched the house.

He killed her. He found the child. He brought the child to safety, against orders, against protocol, against everything he was supposed to be.

The child grew up human, never knowing who had saved her. Her descendants live in Veilfall still. Vincent watches over them from the shadows—not intervening, not revealing himself, just... watching.

It's the only soft thing he allows himself.

[b]CASSANDRA NIGHTFALL[/b]
[i]Duchess of House Nightfall, Commander of the Blood Legions, The Iron Fang[/i]

[b]Public Face:[/b]
Cassandra Nightfall is the Varkyr court's military leader—responsible for defense, for training, for any violence that occurs on a scale larger than Vincent's individual operations. She is known for tactical brilliance, personal combat prowess, and a manner so direct it borders on brutal.

She does not participate in court politics beyond her military role. She does not cultivate allies or play games of influence. She trains, she plans, and when called upon, she destroys. Everything else is irrelevant.

[b]The Truth:[/b]
Cassandra was built for war. She came of age during the Millennium War's final centuries, when Varkyrs needed soldiers more than courtiers. She excelled at violence, rose through merit rather than manipulation, and earned her Duchess title on battlefields where most of her rivals died.

When the war ended, she lost her purpose.

For five hundred years, she has been preparing for a war that might never come. She trains soldiers who may never fight. She plans campaigns against enemies who remain at peace. She is a weapon without a target, slowly going mad from disuse.

She knows this. She cannot change it. War is all she knows, all she is. Without it, she is nothing.

*if (learned_cassandra_origin)
    [b]Origin:[/b]
    Cassandra was Embraced on a battlefield—dying from wounds that should have killed any human, still fighting, still trying to protect soldiers who were already dead.
    
    Her sire was a Knight impressed by her refusal to surrender. He Embraced her without asking, dragging her from death into undeath, transforming a mortal warrior into something that would never stop fighting.
    
    She killed him within a decade. Not from hatred—from respect. He had grown weak, soft, comfortable. The warrior he'd been died long before his body did. She ended what remained, claiming his position and his resources.
    
    She has never regretted the kill. She has occasionally wondered if someone will eventually do the same to her.

*if (learned_cassandra_secrets)
    [b]What She Hides:[/b]
    
    [i]The Fear[/i] — Cassandra is terrified of peace. Not of the concept, but of what it does to her. Without war, without purpose, she feels herself dissolving. Some nights she wakes in a frenzy, attacking shadows, because some part of her cannot accept that there is nothing to fight.
    
    [i]The Letter[/i] — She keeps a letter she never sent, written during the war to a mortal lover who died before it could be delivered. The letter is the last evidence that Cassandra Nightfall was ever capable of softness. She reads it sometimes, trying to remember what it felt like to be the person who wrote those words.
    
    [i]The Lupine Respect[/i] — She has more admiration for Rurik Blackfang than for any Varkyr noble. He is a warrior, like her. He understands honor, duty, the weight of command. In another world, they might have been allies. In this one, they maintain a careful mutual respect that neither will acknowledge publicly.

*if (learned_cassandra_military)
    [b]Military Capabilities:[/b]
    
    [i]The Blood Legions[/i] — Cassandra commands approximately 500 combat-ready Varkyrs, organized into:
    - The Night Guard (50 elite, personal retinue)
    - Five Legions of 90 each (general military force)
    - The Shadow Corps (unknown number, special operations)
    
    [i]Personal Combat[/i] — Cassandra is one of the few Varkyrs who can match Vincent Ashenheart in direct combat. Her style is different—where Vincent uses precision, she uses overwhelming force—but the result is equally lethal.
    
    [i]Tactical Doctrine[/i] — She has developed extensive plans for conflicts against every potential enemy: Lupines, Forsaken, human forces, rogue Varkyrs. These plans are continuously updated, obsessively detailed, and probably unnecessary. She writes them anyway, because it gives her something to do.

[b]CASSANDRA'S CONNECTIONS[/b]

[b]The Matriarch:[/b]
Complicated. Cassandra respects the Matriarch's strength and cunning, but resents her emphasis on peace. The Matriarch keeps Cassandra in reserve, using diplomacy where Cassandra would use force. This is wise. Cassandra knows it's wise. She still chafes under the restraint.

[b]Isabella Crimson:[/b]
Mutual wariness. Isabella represents everything Cassandra isn't—subtle, political, patient. Cassandra finds her unsettling; you can never be certain what Isabella actually thinks. They work together when necessary and avoid each other otherwise.

[b]Vincent Ashenheart:[/b]
Professional respect. They are both weapons; they understand each other on that level. Vincent handles individual problems; Cassandra handles collective problems. Their duties rarely overlap, which is probably good for everyone.

[b]The Soldiers:[/b]
This is Cassandra's actual family. She knows every soldier in the Blood Legions by name, by capability, by history. She has trained most of them personally. She would die for any of them.

They are her purpose when war fails to provide one. Keeping them ready, keeping them alive, keeping them prepared for a conflict that may never come—this is what gets her through the centuries of peace.

[b]Rurik Blackfang:[/b]
Never spoken of, never acknowledged, never acted upon. But when Lupine and Varkyr delegations meet, Cassandra and Rurik often find excuses to speak privately. They discuss tactics, training, the weight of command. Neither would call these conversations friendship. Neither would entirely deny it, either.

[b]RURIK BLACKFANG[/b]
[i]Alpha of Whispering Pines, Voice of the Moon, The Unbroken[/i]

[b]Public Face:[/b]
Rurik Blackfang is the Alpha of Whispering Pines—the leader of the largest and most influential Lupine pack in the region. He is known for strength, honor, and a direct manner that some find refreshing and others find intimidating.

At thirty-eight, he is young for an Alpha—barely past his physical prime by Lupine standards. He earned his position through combat, defeating the previous Alpha in challenge after that Alpha's leadership had grown weak and compromised.

He presents himself as simple: a wolf who leads wolves, who values strength and loyalty, who has no patience for Varkyr games or human complications.

[b]The Truth:[/b]
Rurik is not simple. He is careful.

He knows that the moment he shows complexity, shows doubt, shows anything other than confident strength, his position becomes vulnerable. Alphas who hesitate get challenged. Alphas who philosophize get overthrown. Alphas who admit weakness die.

So he performs simplicity, projecting the archetype of Alpha leadership while internally navigating complexities he can never discuss. The burden of command is heavier than anyone knows—and he carries it alone, because admitting he needs help would be admitting weakness.

*if (learned_rurik_origin)
    [b]Origin:[/b]
    Rurik was born Lupine—one of the rare "pure-bloods" whose parents were both werewolves. He transformed naturally at puberty, without the trauma of a Bite, and was raised in pack traditions from birth.
    
    His father was a respected Hunter; his mother was the previous Alpha's advisor. Both died when Rurik was seventeen—killed by a rogue vampire who crossed into Lupine territory. The pack hunted the vampire down, but the damage was done.
    
    Rurik's hatred of Varkyrs was born that night. It took him twenty years to transform it into something controlled, something useful. The Alpha who helped him through that transformation was the same Alpha he eventually challenged.
    
    He doesn't regret the challenge. He sometimes regrets that it was necessary.

*if (learned_rurik_secrets)
    [b]What He Hides:[/b]
    
    [i]The Doubt[/i] — Rurik is not certain he's the right leader for his pack. He took the position because the previous Alpha had grown weak, because someone had to act, because no one else would. Now, seven years in, he wonders if he is becoming what he replaced.
    
    [i]The Vision[/i] — During his first full moon after becoming Alpha, Rurik saw the Origin Wolf. Not a dream, not a hallucination—the spirit of the First Alpha, appearing in the moonlight, watching him. The Origin Wolf said nothing, asked nothing, simply observed. Rurik has never told anyone about this encounter. He doesn't know what it meant.
    
    [i]The Varkyr Correspondence[/i] — He exchanges letters with Cassandra Nightfall. Nothing treasonous—tactical discussions, debates about leadership, the shared burden of command. But even this innocent correspondence would be damaging if discovered. An Alpha who befriends vampires loses his pack's trust.

*if (learned_rurik_pack)
    [b]The Pack:[/b]
    Whispering Pines contains approximately 200 Lupines:
    
    [i]The Core Pack[/i] — 50 wolves who live permanently in the forest, maintaining the territory and traditions
    
    [i]The Urban Wolves[/i] — 80 wolves who live in Veilfall, maintaining cover identities and pack interests in the city
    
    [i]The Outliers[/i] — 70 wolves who operate independently, checking in periodically but living their own lives
    
    Rurik knows each of them. He has to. An Alpha who doesn't know his pack cannot lead it.

[b]RURIK'S CONNECTIONS[/b]

[b]Luna Silverbrook:[/b]
His Beta. His most trusted advisor. Probably the only person in the pack who sees past his performance of simplicity. She was the first to support his challenge against the old Alpha; she has been beside him ever since.

There's something more there, something neither of them has acknowledged. The pack sees it; everyone sees it. But an Alpha's romantic choices become pack politics, and neither of them is ready for that complication.

[b]Thane Silverpelt:[/b]
His other Beta, chosen for different reasons. Thane is traditional, conservative, suspicious of change. He represents the old guard, the wolves who remember how things were before Rurik. Keeping Thane close keeps the traditionalists appeased.

Rurik doesn't fully trust him. That's fine. Alphas shouldn't fully trust anyone.

[b]The Matriarch:[/b]
He has met her twice, at formal diplomatic functions. Both times, he felt like prey. She is old in a way that makes Rurik's decades feel like minutes. She sees things he cannot perceive, knows things he cannot imagine.

He respects her. He fears her. He will never let either show.

[b]Cassandra Nightfall:[/b]
The strangest relationship in his life. An enemy, technically. A vampire, certainly. And yet... she understands things his own pack cannot. The weight of command. The isolation of leadership. The fear of becoming what you once opposed.

Their correspondence is dangerous. He continues it anyway, because the alternative is complete loneliness.

[b]The Origin Wolf:[/b]
Rurik doesn't know if the vision was real or imagined. He doesn't know what the Origin Wolf wants from him. He only knows that sometimes, in the deepest part of the night, he feels watched—and the watching feels like judgment.

He has tried to be worthy. He doesn't know if he's succeeding.

[b]CAIUS THE UNBOUND[/b]
[i]Founder of the Forsaken Enclave, The First Stable Hybrid, Father of Outcasts[/i]

[b]Public Face:[/b]
Caius is the legendary founder of the Forsaken—the first stable hybrid, the being who transformed "abomination" into "faction." He is approximately 1,500 years old, possesses powers that exceed any other known hybrid, and leads the Forsaken with a combination of wisdom, restraint, and absolute capability.

He speaks rarely, acts decisively, and maintains the Forsaken's position through a combination of diplomatic skill and the implicit threat of what he could do if diplomacy failed.

[b]The Truth:[/b]
Caius is tired.

Fifteen centuries of leading the outcasts. Fifteen centuries of negotiations with factions that consider him an abomination. Fifteen centuries of watching those he's saved die, grow old, make the same mistakes their predecessors made.

He continues because he must. Because the Forsaken need him. Because without his leadership, his stabilization techniques, his institutional knowledge, they would not survive.

But the enthusiasm is gone. The fire that drove him to create a new faction from nothing has faded to embers. He leads from duty now, not passion, and he wonders—increasingly often—how much longer he can sustain even that.

*if (learned_caius_full_history)
    [b]Origin:[/b]
    The public story of Caius's creation—that he was a random experiment who survived—is a lie.
    
    Caius was a human scholar who spent thirty years studying supernatural biology. He theorized that hybridization was possible, that the apparent incompatibility between Varkyr and Lupine was technical rather than fundamental.
    
    When the war's horrors became unbearable, he volunteered himself as a test subject. He designed his own transformation ritual, using blood from the Progenitor's line and essence connected to the Origin Wolf's. He knew he would probably die. He did it anyway.
    
    He has never told this story publicly because it would change how his people see him. They believe he was made a victim and rose above it. The truth—that he chose this, that he engineered his own transformation—would reframe everything.
    
    He doesn't want to be seen as a mad scientist who experimented on himself. He wants to be seen as someone who survived injustice and built something better from the wreckage.

*if (learned_caius_secrets)
    [b]What He Hides:[/b]
    
    [i]The Fatigue[/i] — He is exhausted beyond what any being should have to bear. Centuries of responsibility have worn away everything except the core of duty. Some nights he fantasizes about walking away, finding a quiet place, closing his eyes forever. He never will, but the fantasies grow more frequent.
    
    [i]The Hybrid Limit[/i] — His stabilization techniques don't work for everyone. Approximately 30% of hybrid candidates cannot be safely transformed; the conflicting essences will destroy them no matter what precautions are taken. He has never publicized this, because hope matters more than statistics.
    
    [i]The Gate[/i] — Deep beneath the Undercroft, there is something old. Something that predates the cathedral, predates Veilfall, possibly predates humanity. Caius found it decades ago. He sealed the passage and forbade exploration, and he has never explained why.
    
    He knows what's down there. That's exactly why he sealed it.

*if (learned_caius_powers)
    [b]Capabilities:[/b]
    Caius possesses abilities that no other hybrid has matched:
    
    [i]Dual Transformation[/i] — He can access both full Varkyr form (enhanced vampire abilities) and full Lupine form (complete werewolf transformation) simultaneously. This should be impossible; the forms should conflict. In him, they don't.
    
    [i]Combined Magic[/i] — He can use blood magic and lunar magic together, creating effects that neither tradition can achieve alone. The most dramatic: he can heal others by channeling both energies, regenerating damage that would be fatal even to immortals.
    
    [i]The Unbound State[/i] — In extremis, Caius can enter a state where all limitations fall away. He has done this exactly once, during the assault on the Undercroft four centuries ago. He killed fifty Varkyrs in minutes, and the power nearly destroyed him afterward.
    
    He hopes he never has to do it again. He knows he will, if the Forsaken are threatened.

[b]CAIUS'S CONNECTIONS[/b]

[b]The Forsaken:[/b]
They are his children, in every sense that matters. He has watched generations of them live and die. He has stabilized hundreds personally, guiding them through transformations that would have killed them without his help.

He loves them, in his exhausted way. He will protect them until his final breath. He has simply... run out of the energy to express that love with warmth rather than duty.

[b]Vale:[/b]
His current protege—a young hybrid who shows exceptional potential. Caius sees in Vale what he once saw in himself: intelligence, determination, the capacity to lead.

He is training Vale as a potential successor, though he has never said this explicitly. If anything happens to Caius, Vale will need to be ready. The Forsaken will need someone to follow.

[b]The Void-Walker's Shadow:[/b]
Caius knows more about the entity that created the Progenitor than almost anyone living. His research, before his transformation, uncovered fragments of the Void-Walker's history—enough to suspect that the being's influence didn't end with the Progenitor's creation.

He believes the Void-Walker is still active, still watching, still pursuing goals that span millennia. He has no proof. But the Gate beneath the Undercroft whispers sometimes, in a voice that sounds like silence made audible.

That's why he sealed it. That's why he will never open it again.

[b]ELENA VOSS[/b]
[i]Inquisitor of the Silver Legion, Hunter of Monsters, The Burning Faith[/i]

[b]Public Face:[/b]
Elena Voss is one of the Silver Legion's most effective hunters—a woman who has killed more rogue supernaturals than anyone else currently active. She is known for devotion to her mission, combat skill that rivals supernatural beings, and an intensity that some find inspiring and others find unsettling.

She operates with official Church sanction, carries blessed weapons, and has never failed to eliminate a designated target.

[b]The Truth:[/b]
Elena's faith is genuine, but it is not the serene faith of comfortable believers. It is the desperate faith of someone who needs something to believe in or she will fall apart.

Before she joined the Legion, she was... something else. Someone else. That person died the night her family was killed by a vampire who fed from them for entertainment, who kept Elena alive just long enough to watch everyone she loved die.

The Legion found her afterward, broken and purposeless. They gave her training, weapons, and a cause. They told her that the monsters could be fought. They showed her how.

She rebuilt herself around that purpose. Without it, there is nothing.

*if (learned_elena_origin)
    [b]Origin:[/b]
    Elena Voss was born to a merchant family in the outskirts of Veilfall—close enough to the city for comfort, far enough for the supernatural factions to consider the area "unwatched."

[b]ELENA'S CONNECTIONS[/b]

[b]The Silver Legion:[/b]
Her family, her purpose, her identity. Without the Legion, Elena would be a broken survivor with nothing to live for. They gave her meaning; she has given them everything in return.

[b]The Church:[/b]
Her faith is real but complicated. She believes in God, in divine protection, in the sanctity of human life. She is less certain about the Church as an institution—she has seen too much political maneuvering, too many compromises with the supernatural factions the Church supposedly opposes.

[i]Vale[/i] — The Forsaken has helped Elena on two separate occasions, intervening against rogues who threatened humans. Elena doesn't know what to do with this information.
